Farmer's Step-by-Step Guide to Going Organic

Actionable transition framework for Kenyan smallholders utilizing PCPB 109 biopesticides, ICIPE 20, and soil health management.

Step 1: Soil Pre-Drench

Apply Trichoderma harzianum soil drench to suppress Fusarium wilt, Pythium, and root rot before transplanting.

Step 2: Neemol & Bt Spray

Substitute synthetic organophosphates with Azadirachtin 0.03% EC (Neemol) and Bacillus thuringiensis for caterpillar control.

Step 3: Pheromone Traps

Deploy Tuta absoluta male moth pheromone lures. Monitor weekly (threshold: 2–3 moths/trap/week trigger).

Step 4: KOAN PGS Seal

Join local KOAN PGS farmer clusters (1,634 active farmers) and enforce 14-day zero-synthetic Pre-Harvest Intervals.

🔥 Safe Cooking & Preparation Procedures
1. Sort & Trim: Remove outer bruised leaves of kale and cabbage where systemic surface residues collect.
2. Soak: Submerge produce in 1:3 vinegar or 2% salt water solution for 10 full minutes.
3. Peel Skins: Peel outer skins of tomatoes and carrots to remove lipophilic compounds.
4. Cook/Boil: Thermal cooking further breaks down heat-labile organophosphates before serving.

Simple Step-by-Step Food Safety Guide / Mwongozo Rahisi wa Usalama wa Chakula

Clear, easy-to-understand explanations of pesticide safety, household washing, and organic farming for all community members.

1. What are Pesticide Residues?

Pesticides are chemical sprays used by farmers to protect crops from insects. Small trace amounts can stay on unwashed vegetables.

2. Why Washing Works (60-70%)

Soaking vegetables in salt water or vinegar dissolves surface chemical spots, removing 6 to 7 out of every 10 chemical spots!

3. Why Organic is Safer

Organic farming uses natural neem extracts and biological micro-organisms. No synthetic chemicals touch your food!
